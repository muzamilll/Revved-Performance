### "New to tuning?" Explainer
Modern car engines are controlled by a computer called an ECU. When a car leaves the factory, the manufacturer deliberately limits the engine's power in this computer software. They do this to meet different tax brackets, account for poor fuel quality in other countries, or artificially separate different models that actually share the exact same engine. 

ECU remapping (often just called "tuning" or a "remap") is the process of safely rewriting that software. By adjusting parameters like fuel pressure and ignition timing, we remove those artificial factory limits. The result is a car that is sharper, more responsive, and pulls much harder when you accelerate. We tailor the software specifically to your car, ensuring it stays well within the safe working limits of the engine.

### Glossary
- **ECU**: The Engine Control Unit—the computer brain that controls how your engine runs.
- **Remap**: Safely rewriting the ECU's software to unlock more power and better response.
- **Stage 1**: A custom tune designed for a completely standard car with no upgraded parts.
- **Stage 2**: A more aggressive tune requiring upgraded hardware like a free-flowing exhaust or intercooler.
- **DSG**: A type of fast-shifting automatic gearbox used heavily by VW, Audi, Seat, and Skoda.
- **DPF**: Diesel Particulate Filter—an exhaust component on diesel cars that catches soot.
- **OBD vs bench**: OBD means we tune the car by plugging a laptop into the dashboard port; "bench" means we temporarily plug directly into the ECU itself.
